## Research Delta: exact reconstruction can use an imperfect predictor

The proposal makes a complete lossless archive the consumer of learned relationships. A stored residual corrects a predictor's error; the predictor itself need not be invertible. Exact reconstruction instead requires identical deterministic predictions, retained context and metadata, and defined arithmetic behavior. A poor predictor increases residual cost rather than justifying removal of unexplained detail. Library, model, framing, residual and adaptation costs all belong in the comparison.

For Continuum, exact retained artifacts and a task-sufficient summary are different contracts. A summary can be useful without reconstructing its source. If a future representation does promise exact recovery, an unavailable context, nondeterministic prediction or unretained result must not be hidden behind a digest. Our current source objects remain exact, while context packs explicitly select and omit material; this reading does not change those semantics or implement a new codec.

The source also distinguishes training-frozen reusable structure from synthesis that sees each complete file. Both can be useful compression mechanisms, but they answer different generalization questions. A future continuity comparison should similarly record what each successor can see and when, including paid retrieval and retained state.

## Research Delta: algebraic equivalence is not a second experimental arm

`BETA_RADIX.md` constructs an exact geometric positional system on a triangular lattice. Its residue classes, quotient and decreasing integer norm supply a termination/uniqueness argument; the saved finite box checks a bounded implementation, not the general theorem by enumeration. The construction is explicitly different from ordinary positive-real base 12 and makes no novelty, native-speed or universal-radix claim.

Two geometric places address 144 residues, the same address count as one ordinary twelve-way digit in each of two coordinates. That count alone adds no compression benefit. A factorization into index-three and index-four steps with identical grouping, prediction and coding is another spelling of the same code. To compare useful alternatives, change a specified policy or implementation cost rather than count equivalent formulations as competing evidence.

This transfers directly to Continuum's evaluations: two encodings or projections may differ syntactically while exposing the same decision-relevant information and work. First state the equivalence relation, then identify the intervention capable of changing the outcome. Scalar radix, quantization, geometry, learned representation and execution layout are separate factors. No geometric or arithmetic convention becomes a Continuum requirement through this review.

## Research Delta: a canonical representation can introduce preferences

The source's finite digit alphabet has an orientation preference even though the underlying lattice has rotational symmetry. Its cardinality argument and exact reconstruction do not establish locality, regular tile shapes, short carries or better compression. A layout can preserve all values while changing how a bounded consumer encounters them.

Continuum should likewise evaluate canonical ordering against the consumer's decisions and retrieval costs. Stable identity is useful, but stability alone does not establish an unbiased or optimal context presentation. Redundancy or extra frame/state information has a cost that must be counted. This is a design lesson, not evidence that the current context selector has a measured ordering defect.
